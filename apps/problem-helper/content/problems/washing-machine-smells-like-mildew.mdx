# Washing Machine Smells Like Mildew: Complete Odor Removal Guide

Opening your washing machine to a blast of musty, mildew odor is unpleasant and concerning. Worse, this smell transfers to freshly washed clothes, defeating the entire purpose of laundry. A mildew smell indicates bacterial and mold growth inside your washer—a surprisingly common problem in both front-load and top-load machines. The good news: it's fixable with proper cleaning and preventive maintenance.

## Why Washing Machines Smell Like Mildew

Washing machines create perfect conditions for mold and mildew growth: moisture, warmth, darkness, and organic matter (dirt, detergent residue, fabric fibers). When water doesn't fully drain or evaporate between loads, microorganisms thrive in hidden areas.

**Common causes include:**

**Residual moisture**: Modern high-efficiency washers use less water. Combined with airtight door seals on front-loaders, moisture gets trapped inside, creating humidity that feeds mold growth.

**Detergent buildup**: Excess detergent doesn't rinse away completely, leaving residue that traps bacteria and mold. Ironically, using too much detergent trying to eliminate smells makes the problem worse.

**Door seal contamination**: Front-load washers have rubber gaskets around the door that trap water, lint, hair, and soap residue—prime mold breeding grounds.

**Drain issues**: Partially clogged drains prevent complete water evacuation, leaving standing water that becomes stagnant and smelly.

**Infrequent use**: Washers that sit unused for weeks develop smells from trapped moisture with no fresh water circulation.

Front-load washers are more prone to mildew smell than top-loaders because their horizontal drum and tight door seal trap moisture more effectively. However, top-loaders aren't immune—especially newer high-efficiency models.

## Deep Cleaning Your Washing Machine

### Method 1: Vinegar and Baking Soda Cleaning (Most Effective)

This natural method kills bacteria, dissolves residue, and neutralizes odors without harsh chemicals.

**Steps:**
1. Empty the washer completely
2. Wipe down the door seal and remove visible debris
3. Add 2 cups white vinegar to the detergent dispenser
4. Run the hottest, longest cycle available (empty)
5. Pause mid-cycle and let vinegar sit for 1 hour
6. Resume and complete the cycle
7. Sprinkle 1/2 cup baking soda directly into the drum
8. Run another hot cycle (short cycle is fine)
9. Wipe down all surfaces with vinegar-soaked cloth
10. Leave door open to air dry completely

Vinegar kills 99% of bacteria and dissolves mineral deposits. Baking soda neutralizes acidic odors and provides gentle abrasive cleaning. This combination attacks mildew from multiple angles.

**Frequency:** Monthly for prevention, weekly if smell is severe.

### Method 2: Bleach Cleaning (For Severe Mold)

Bleach is more aggressive and effective against heavy mold growth, but use carefully—it can damage rubber seals with frequent use.

**Steps:**
1. Ensure excellent ventilation (bleach fumes are strong)
2. Add 1/2 cup bleach to the detergent dispenser (never pour directly into drum with colored clothes nearby)
3. Run the hottest, longest cycle empty
4. Run a second rinse cycle with plain water
5. Wipe all surfaces thoroughly
6. Air dry with door open

**Warning:** Never mix bleach with vinegar or other cleaners—this creates toxic chlorine gas. If you used vinegar previously, run a plain water cycle first.

**Frequency:** Monthly maximum—excessive bleach deteriorates rubber seals.

### Method 3: Commercial Washer Cleaning Tablets

Several brands (Affresh, Tide, OxiClean) make tablets specifically for washing machine cleaning. These contain concentrated cleaning agents designed to dissolve residue and kill odor-causing bacteria.

**Steps:**
1. Follow package directions (typically one tablet per cycle)
2. Place tablet directly in empty drum
3. Run hot water cycle as directed
4. Wipe surfaces after cycle completes
5. Leave door open to dry

Commercial cleaners are convenient but more expensive than DIY methods. They work well for maintenance but may require multiple treatments for severe odors.

## Cleaning Specific Problem Areas

### Door Seal/Gasket (Front-Load Washers)

The rubber gasket is the #1 mold breeding ground in front-loaders.

**Detailed cleaning:**
1. Pull back the rubber seal to expose hidden areas
2. You'll likely find standing water, black mold, hair, and gunk
3. Spray with vinegar or bleach solution (1:1 with water)
4. Scrub thoroughly with an old toothbrush
5. Wipe clean with microfiber cloth
6. Dry completely, paying attention to folds and crevices
7. For stubborn mold, make a paste of baking soda and water, apply, let sit 15 minutes, scrub

**Severe mold:** If mold has deeply stained or damaged the seal, replacement may be necessary ($100-200 professionally installed).

### Detergent Dispenser

Liquid detergent leaves sticky residue that traps bacteria.

**Cleaning:**
1. Remove the dispenser drawer completely (consult manual—usually pulls straight out or has a release button)
2. Soak in hot water with vinegar for 30 minutes
3. Scrub with a small brush to remove buildup
4. Clean the dispenser housing inside the washer with vinegar-soaked cloth and toothbrush
5. Rinse thoroughly and dry before reinstalling

### Drain Filter/Pump

Most front-loaders and some top-loaders have an accessible drain filter that catches debris before it reaches the pump.

**Location:** Usually behind a small panel at the bottom front of the washer.

**Cleaning:**
1. Place towels underneath (water will spill)
2. Open access panel
3. Slowly unscrew the filter (expect water to pour out)
4. Remove trapped debris (coins, hair, lint, small items)
5. Rinse filter under running water
6. Wipe filter housing clean
7. Reinstall filter securely

**Frequency:** Every 3-6 months or when drainage seems slow.

### Interior Drum

Even though you can't see much buildup, the drum accumulates residue.

**Cleaning:**
1. After running cleaning cycle, inspect drum for visible residue
2. Wipe down with vinegar and water (1:1 solution)
3. For stubborn spots, make baking soda paste and scrub
4. Pay attention to drum holes where water enters
5. Use a flashlight to check for hidden mold in crevices

## Eliminating the Smell From Clothes

If clothes smell like mildew even after cleaning the washer:

1. **Rewash affected items** with 1 cup white vinegar added to rinse cycle
2. **Use hot water** (check garment care labels first)
3. **Add baking soda** to the wash cycle (1/2 cup) for odor absorption
4. **Dry immediately** after washing—don't let wet clothes sit
5. **Sun dry when possible**—UV rays kill bacteria naturally
6. **For stubborn smells**, soak clothes in vinegar water (1:4 ratio) for 1 hour before washing

Sometimes mildew smell has penetrated towels or workout clothes so deeply that multiple treatments are necessary. Don't give up after one attempt.

## Prevention: Stop Mildew From Returning

### After Every Load

**Leave the door open:** This is the single most important preventive step. Prop front-loader doors open 2-3 inches. Top-loaders can stay fully open. This allows moisture to evaporate instead of creating humidity inside.

**Wipe the door seal:** Quick 10-second wipe with a dry towel removes standing water from the gasket.

**Remove clothes immediately:** Wet clothes sitting in the washer create moisture and provide organic material for bacteria. Transfer to dryer or hang within 10 minutes of cycle ending.

### Weekly Maintenance

**Run a hot water cycle:** Even if you normally wash in cold water, run one hot cycle weekly to kill bacteria and dissolve detergent residue.

**Wipe surfaces:** Quick wipe-down of door seal, drum, and detergent dispenser prevents buildup.

### Monthly Maintenance

**Deep clean:** Run vinegar or commercial cleaner cycle monthly.

**Check drain filter:** Ensure filter isn't clogged with debris.

**Inspect hoses:** Look for standing water in drain hose or visible kinks restricting drainage.

### Proper Detergent Use

**Use HE detergent** in high-efficiency washers—regular detergent creates excess suds that don't rinse completely.

**Measure correctly:** Use less than you think. Most people use 2-3 times the necessary amount. Follow the dispenser fill lines or bottle instructions for your load size.

**Liquid vs. powder:** Powder detergent rinses more completely, reducing residue. If using liquid, use minimal amounts.

**Avoid fabric softener in every load:** Fabric softener leaves waxy coating that traps bacteria. Use sparingly or switch to dryer sheets.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How often should I clean my washing machine?**

Run a cleaning cycle monthly for prevention. If you notice any odor, clean immediately and increase frequency to weekly until smell is eliminated, then maintain monthly.

**Q: Can I use essential oils to make my washer smell better?**

While essential oils (tea tree, lavender, eucalyptus) have antibacterial properties, they don't replace proper cleaning. Use them as a supplement—add 5-10 drops to the final rinse of a cleaning cycle for pleasant scent, but still address the underlying mold/bacteria cause.

**Q: Is mildew smell harmful?**

The mold and bacteria causing the smell can trigger allergies, respiratory issues, and skin irritation in sensitive individuals. Additionally, these microorganisms transfer to your "clean" clothes, potentially causing health issues.

**Q: Why does my brand new washer smell like mildew?**

New washers sometimes come from factories with test water sitting inside. More commonly, new washer smell develops quickly if you close the door immediately after installation without allowing initial moisture to evaporate. Always leave doors open between uses from day one.

**Q: Will running empty hot cycles waste water and energy?**

Yes, but the cost is minimal—about $1-2 per cleaning cycle. Compare this to replacing a $600-1000 washing machine prematurely due to mold damage, or constantly rewashing clothes that smell musty. The small energy cost is worthwhile prevention.

**Q: My washer still smells after cleaning. What now?**

If smell persists after multiple cleaning treatments: (1) Check drain pump and hose for clogs; (2) Inspect behind/under the washer for mold growth on exterior; (3) Consider that smell might be from nearby sources (walls, drain pipes) rather than the washer itself; (4) Call a professional to inspect internal components you can't access.

## When to Call a Professional

Contact an appliance repair technician if:

- Smell persists after thorough cleaning and maintenance
- You suspect mold behind the drum or in internal components
- Drain issues suggest pump or hose problems
- Door seal damage requires replacement
- Washer doesn't drain completely (clog in drain system)
- You find extensive mold growth you can't safely clean

Professional washer cleaning and inspection typically costs $100-200 but can identify problems beyond DIY scope.

## Special Considerations for Front-Load Washers

Front-loaders require more diligent maintenance due to their design:

**Critical habits:**
- Never close the door immediately after use
- Wipe gasket after every load
- Remove detergent residue weekly
- Use minimal detergent (HE formula only)
- Run monthly cleaning cycles religiously

Many people regret buying front-loaders due to mildew problems, but the issue is 100% preventable with proper habits. Front-loaders aren't defective—they're just less forgiving of poor maintenance.

## Conclusion

Mildew smell in washing machines is fixable and preventable. The combination of regular cleaning (vinegar/baking soda or commercial cleaner monthly), proper detergent use, and most importantly—leaving the door open between loads—eliminates the problem.

If you're currently battling mildew smell, commit to the deep cleaning process and new maintenance habits for 4-6 weeks. The smell will disappear and stay gone with consistent prevention.

Your washing machine should produce fresh-smelling, truly clean laundry. With these strategies, it will—and you'll extend your washer's lifespan while improving your laundry results dramatically.
