# Oven Not Heating to Correct Temperature: Calibration and Repair

An oven that doesn't heat to the correct temperature ruins recipes and wastes time. Temperature variations of 25-50°F are surprisingly common—your oven might read 350°F but actually run at 320°F or 380°F. This happens due to thermostat calibration drift, faulty temperature sensors, aging heating elements, or door seal problems. The difference between calibration issues (easily fixable) and component failure (requires parts) is testing actual temperature versus set temperature. This guide covers testing oven accuracy and fixing both simple and complex temperature problems.

## Testing Oven Accuracy

**Before attempting repairs, confirm oven is actually inaccurate.**

### Using Oven Thermometer

**Most reliable method:**

**What you need:**
- Oven thermometer ($10-20)
- Available at any kitchen store

**Testing procedure:**

1. Place oven thermometer on center rack
2. Preheat oven to 350°F
3. Once preheated (usually signals when done), wait additional 10 minutes
4. Read thermometer without opening door if possible (use oven light)
5. Compare thermometer reading to set temperature

**Repeat test at multiple temperatures:**
- 250°F
- 350°F
- 425°F

**Results interpretation:**

**Consistent offset:**
- Example: Set 350°F, reads 325°F every time (25°F low)
- Solution: Calibration adjustment

**Inconsistent temperatures:**
- Example: Varies widely (340°F to 380°F at 350°F setting)
- Solution: Faulty thermostat or sensor, needs replacement

**Way off (50+ degrees):**
- Example: Set 350°F, reads 280°F or 420°F
- Solution: Serious component failure

### Observing Cooking Results

**Practical indicators:**

- Cookies spread too much: Oven running cool
- Cookies burn on bottom: Oven too hot or rack too low
- Cakes don't rise properly: Usually too cool
- Food burns outside, raw inside: Too hot
- Everything takes longer: Running cool
- Everything cooks faster: Running hot

These symptoms support thermometer testing.

## Simple Fix: Oven Calibration

**If oven is consistently off by 10-35°F**, calibration usually fixes it.

**Most ovens have calibration adjustment** allowing temperature offset.

### Digital/Electronic Ovens

**Process varies by brand but generally:**

**Common method:**

1. Press and hold two buttons simultaneously (varies: often "Bake" + "Broil" or similar)
2. Display shows current calibration offset (usually 0)
3. Use number pad or +/- buttons to adjust offset
4. If oven runs 25°F cool, enter +25
5. If oven runs 25°F hot, enter -25
6. Press "Start" or "Set" to save
7. Retest with oven thermometer

**Brand-specific procedures:**

**GE ovens:**
- Press "Bake" and "Broil" together for 3 seconds
- Adjust using number pad
- Press "Start" to save

**Whirlpool:**
- Settings menu → Calibration → Adjust → Save

**Samsung:**
- Settings → Oven Calibration → Adjust

**LG:**
- Settings → Temperature Adjustment

**Frigidaire:**
- Press and hold "Bake" for 5 seconds
- Use +/- to adjust

**Always consult your oven manual** for specific instructions—process varies.

### Mechanical/Dial Ovens

Older ovens with dial controls often have calibration screw:

1. Pull off temperature knob
2. Look for calibration screw on back of knob or behind it on shaft
3. Loosen screw slightly (usually requires small screwdriver)
4. Rotate disc to adjust calibration
5. Tighten screw
6. Replace knob
7. Test

**Note:** Some dial ovens cannot be calibrated—just note offset and adjust set temperature mentally.

### Limits of Calibration

**Most ovens allow +/- 35°F adjustment maximum.**

If your oven is off by more than 35°F, calibration won't fully correct it—component replacement needed.

## Component Failures and Repairs

### Faulty Temperature Sensor (RTD Sensor)

**Electric ovens** use temperature sensor that can fail.

**Symptoms:**
- Oven temperature way off
- Fluctuates wildly
- Error codes on display (F3, F4 on many models)
- Oven won't heat at all

**Testing sensor:**

**You need:** Multimeter

1. Locate sensor (usually rear oven wall, thin metal probe)
2. Unplug oven (safety first)
3. Remove sensor (usually one screw)
4. Test resistance with multimeter
5. At room temp (70°F), should read 1,000-1,100 ohms
6. If reading is way off or infinite: Sensor failed

**Replacement:**

1. Order replacement sensor for your oven model ($20-60)
2. Unplug oven
3. Remove old sensor (one screw, pull out connector)
4. Insert new sensor
5. Reconnect wiring
6. Secure with screw
7. Test oven

**DIY difficulty:** Easy—15 minutes

**Professional service:** $150-250 installed

### Failing Heating Elements (Electric Ovens)

**Symptoms:**
- Oven heats very slowly
- Doesn't reach temperature
- Element doesn't glow red
- Visible breaks or blisters in element

**Testing elements:**

**Visual test:**
1. Turn on oven to 350°F
2. Watch elements (bake element on bottom, broil on top)
3. Should glow red within minutes
4. If one doesn't glow or glows unevenly: Failed

**Resistance test:**
1. Unplug oven
2. Remove element (usually held by 2 screws)
3. Test resistance with multimeter
4. Should read 15-50 ohms depending on element
5. Infinite resistance: Element burned out

**Replacement:**

1. Order correct replacement element for your model ($30-80)
2. Unplug oven
3. Remove screws holding old element
4. Pull element forward and disconnect wires
5. Connect new element wires
6. Secure new element with screws
7. Test

**DIY difficulty:** Easy to moderate—30 minutes

**Professional service:** $200-300 installed

### Gas Oven Igniter Weak

**Gas ovens** use igniter to open gas valve.

**Symptoms:**
- Oven takes very long to preheat
- Oven doesn't heat to full temperature
- Igniter glows but gas doesn't ignite immediately
- Clicking without ignition

**Diagnosis:**
1. Turn on oven
2. Watch igniter through oven window
3. Should glow bright orange/white and ignite gas within 90 seconds
4. If glows dim or takes forever to ignite: Weak igniter

**Replacement:**

1. Order replacement igniter for your model ($30-70)
2. Turn off gas supply to oven
3. Unplug oven
4. Remove oven bottom panel
5. Disconnect wire from old igniter
6. Remove screws holding igniter
7. Install new igniter
8. Reconnect wiring
9. Test

**Warning:** Working with gas appliances requires care. If uncomfortable, hire professional.

**Professional service:** $200-350 installed

### Thermostat Failure

**Symptoms:**
- Temperature wildly inaccurate
- Cannot be calibrated into range
- Constant cycling on/off
- Oven overheats

**Solution:**
Thermostat replacement requires professional service in most cases. Control boards can be expensive ($150-400 for part alone).

## Door Seal and Heat Loss Issues

**Poor door seal allows heat escape**, making oven run cool.

**Testing door seal:**

**Dollar bill test:**
1. Open oven door
2. Place dollar bill against door frame
3. Close door on bill
4. Try pulling bill out
5. Should have resistance all around door
6. If slides out easily anywhere: Seal is poor in that area

**Visual inspection:**
1. Examine door gasket (rubber seal around opening)
2. Look for cracks, tears, compression set (flattened areas)
3. Check for gaps when door closed
4. Ensure door closes fully and latch works

**Replacement:**

1. Order replacement gasket for your model ($30-80)
2. Remove old gasket (usually just pulls out of groove)
3. Clean groove thoroughly
4. Install new gasket into groove (may need to press firmly)
5. Ensure corners seated properly
6. Test door closure

**DIY difficulty:** Easy—20 minutes

## Additional Temperature Problems

### Uneven Heating

**Symptoms:**
- Food cooks unevenly
- Hot spots in oven
- One side burns while other side undercooked

**Causes:**

**Convection fan failure** (in convection ovens):
- Fan should run during convection cooking
- If silent or not spinning: Motor or fan blade issue

**Blocked vents:**
- Ensure no foil blocking vents
- Clear any debris from vent holes

**Warped racks:**
- Replace bent or warped racks
- Ensure racks properly seated

**Solutions:**
- Use convection mode if available
- Rotate food halfway through cooking
- Use middle rack for most even heat
- Don't overcrowd oven

### Oven Won't Maintain Temperature

**Cycles on and off excessively:**

**Possible causes:**
- Faulty sensor
- Door seal leak
- Thermostat malfunction
- Control board issue

**Requires diagnosis** of specific component failure.

## Preventive Maintenance

**Keep oven accurate:**

**Monthly:**
- Wipe up spills immediately
- Clean oven regularly (buildup affects heat distribution)
- Check door closes properly

**Annually:**
- Test temperature accuracy with oven thermometer
- Inspect door seal for wear
- Recalibrate if needed
- Have professional inspection for gas ovens

**Avoid:**
- Lining oven bottom with foil (blocks airflow and heat)
- Slamming door (damages seal and calibration)
- Using oven for storage (risks damage to components)

## When to Call Professional

**DIY appropriate for:**
- Temperature calibration
- Oven thermometer testing
- Element replacement (if comfortable)
- Sensor replacement (if comfortable)
- Door seal replacement

**Call professional for:**
- Gas oven igniter issues (if uncomfortable with gas)
- Control board replacement
- Thermostat replacement
- Self-clean lock mechanism issues
- Any unfamiliar electrical work
- When error codes appear you can't diagnose

**Service call cost:** $100-150 diagnostic + parts and labor

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How accurate should my oven be?**

+/- 25°F is considered acceptable. Ideally +/- 15°F or better.

**Q: Does using aluminum foil affect oven temperature?**

Yes, if it blocks vents or covers entire rack/bottom. Use foil for food only, not lining oven.

**Q: My oven temperature is accurate but food still cooks wrong. Why?**

Rack position matters hugely. Try middle rack. Also ensure proper preheating and don't overcrowd.

**Q: Can I calibrate oven without thermometer?**

Not accurately. Invest in $15 oven thermometer—essential tool for any baker.

**Q: How often should I recalibrate my oven?**

Test annually. Recalibrate only if it drifts beyond acceptable range.

**Q: Will self-cleaning affect oven temperature accuracy?**

Can temporarily affect it due to extreme heat. If major calibration shifts after self-clean, may indicate failing components.

## Conclusion

Oven temperature inaccuracy of 25-35°F is fixable through simple calibration adjustment, a process accessible through settings on digital ovens or calibration screw on mechanical models. Always test actual temperature with oven thermometer before assuming oven is broken—knowing the specific offset guides whether calibration or component replacement is needed.

For ovens off by more than 35°F or showing inconsistent temperatures, component failure is likely. Temperature sensors and heating elements are common culprits and relatively easy DIY replacements for those comfortable with basic appliance repair. Gas oven igniters often weaken over time, causing slow preheating and incomplete heating.

Door seals affect temperature significantly yet are often overlooked. A simple dollar bill test identifies seal problems, and replacement gaskets cost $30-80 with straightforward installation.

Annual temperature testing with oven thermometer catches problems early before they ruin multiple batches of food. This simple habit, combined with prompt calibration adjustments, keeps ovens cooking accurately for years.
