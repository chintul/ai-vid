# Toilet Tank Not Filling: Fix Fill Valve and Water Flow Issues

A toilet tank that won't fill or fills very slowly prevents proper flushing and leaves your toilet unusable. Most filling problems stem from closed water supply valves, clogged fill valves, failed float mechanisms, or water supply line issues rather than complex plumbing problems. The fill valve—the mechanism that controls water flow into the tank—is the most common culprit and is inexpensive and straightforward to replace. This guide systematically diagnoses why your toilet tank isn't filling and provides step-by-step fixes.

## Quick Diagnosis Checklist

**Start with these simple checks:**

**1. Is water supply valve open?**
- Locate valve on wall behind toilet (near floor)
- Turn counterclockwise to fully open
- Surprisingly common cause—valve partially closed

**2. Does tank fill at all, just slowly?**
- Fills slowly: Clogged fill valve or low water pressure
- Doesn't fill at all: Closed valve, failed fill valve, or no water supply

**3. Do you hear water running?**
- Hissing sound: Water flowing but may be going straight to overflow tube
- No sound: No water entering tank

**4. Is float stuck or damaged?**
- Float ball or cup should move freely
- If stuck in "up" position, fill valve thinks tank is full

**5. When did problem start?**
- After recent flush: Fill valve or flapper issue
- After plumbing work: Supply valve or water pressure
- Gradually: Mineral buildup in fill valve

## Simple Fixes

### Fix 1: Open Water Supply Valve

**Most common cause** of toilet not filling.

1. Locate shut-off valve on wall behind toilet (usually left side)
2. Turn valve counterclockwise (lefty-loosey)
3. Turn until completely open (can't turn anymore)
4. Flush toilet and observe tank filling

**Note:** Valves can be accidentally bumped closed during cleaning or other work.

### Fix 2: Check Water Supply to House

If toilet was working and suddenly stopped:

1. Check if other faucets in house have water
2. If no water anywhere: Main water supply issue or outage
3. If only toilet affected: Continue troubleshooting toilet-specific issues

### Fix 3: Adjust or Replace Float

**Float tells fill valve when tank is full.**

**Ball float (older toilets):**
1. Lift float arm while tank is filling
2. Water should stop flowing when lifted
3. If doesn't stop: Fill valve problem
4. If float is waterlogged (feels heavy): Replace float ball
5. Unscrew old ball from arm, screw on new one ($5-10)
6. Adjust float height by bending float arm slightly

**Cup float (newer toilets):**
1. Check that float cup moves freely on fill valve shaft
2. Ensure nothing blocking float movement
3. Adjust float height using adjustment clip on fill valve
4. Move clip up for more water, down for less

## Clean or Replace Fill Valve

**Fill valve (also called ballcock)** controls water entry into tank.

### Cleaning Fill Valve

**For slow filling due to mineral buildup:**

**Supplies needed:**
- White vinegar
- Old toothbrush
- Bucket
- Pliers (if needed)

**Steps:**

1. Turn off water supply valve
2. Flush toilet to empty tank
3. Remove fill valve cap (usually twist or clips)
4. Look for debris or mineral deposits in valve
5. Clean with old toothbrush
6. Rinse parts with vinegar
7. Check small screen/filter in valve for clogs
8. Flush out debris
9. Reassemble
10. Turn water back on slowly
11. Test

**For persistent clogs:** Replacement usually easier than thorough cleaning.

### Replacing Fill Valve

**When to replace:**
- Cleaning doesn't improve flow
- Valve is over 5 years old
- Visible cracks or damage
- Won't shut off properly

**New fill valve cost:** $10-30

**Tools needed:**
- Adjustable pliers or wrench
- Bucket or towels
- New fill valve (Fluidmaster 400A is universal fit)

**Replacement process:**

**Step 1: Turn off water and empty tank**
1. Turn off water supply valve
2. Flush toilet to drain tank
3. Sponge or towel out remaining water

**Step 2: Disconnect water supply line**
1. Place bucket under connection
2. Use pliers to loosen nut connecting water line to bottom of tank
3. Remove supply line (water will drip—normal)

**Step 3: Remove old fill valve**
1. Inside tank: Disconnect overflow tube (small tube) from fill valve
2. Unscrew float (if attached)
3. Hold bottom of fill valve with one hand (under tank)
4. Unscrew large plastic nut on bottom with other hand
5. Lift fill valve out of tank

**Step 4: Install new fill valve**
1. Adjust height of new valve to fit your tank (per instructions)
2. Insert valve through hole in tank bottom
3. Ensure rubber gasket is properly positioned
4. Thread and hand-tighten mounting nut from underneath
5. Ensure valve is straight
6. Tighten nut an additional 1/2 turn with pliers (don't over-tighten)

**Step 5: Reconnect water supply**
1. Reconnect water supply line to bottom of fill valve
2. Hand-tighten, then 1/2 turn more with pliers

**Step 6: Connect overflow tube**
1. Attach refill tube (small tube) from fill valve to overflow pipe
2. Use clip provided to secure

**Step 7: Adjust float height**
1. Set float for water level about 1 inch below top of overflow tube
2. Test and adjust as needed

**Step 8: Turn on water and test**
1. Slowly open water supply valve
2. Watch for leaks at connections
3. Allow tank to fill
4. Adjust float if needed
5. Flush several times to verify proper operation

**Total time:** 30-45 minutes for first-time DIYer

## Other Potential Issues

### Clogged Water Supply Line

**Symptoms:**
- Slow filling
- Reduced water pressure
- Problem started suddenly

**Testing:**

1. Turn off water valve
2. Disconnect supply line from bottom of tank
3. Place end in bucket
4. Turn on water valve
5. Observe water flow from line
6. Should be strong stream
7. If weak: Supply line is clogged or kinked

**Solutions:**
- Straighten kinked line
- Replace supply line ($10-20 for braided stainless steel)
- Check for shut-off valve partially closed

### Flapper Not Sealing (Water Draining Faster Than Filling)

**Symptoms:**
- Water runs constantly
- Tank never reaches full level
- Phantom flushes (refills randomly)

**Diagnosis:**
Add food coloring to tank. If color appears in bowl without flushing, flapper is leaking.

**Fix:**
Replace flapper ($5-10)—simple fix taking 10 minutes

### Low Water Pressure to House

**Affects all fixtures:**

If all faucets have low pressure:
- Check main shut-off valve fully open
- Check pressure regulator if house has one
- May need plumber to diagnose municipal supply issues

If only toilet affected:
- Problem is toilet-specific (fill valve, supply line, shut-off valve)

### Broken Trip Lever/Flush Handle

**Symptoms:**
- Flush handle broken or disconnected
- Chain disconnected from flapper
- Tank won't empty when flushed

**Note:** This prevents tank from emptying, but doesn't directly prevent filling. However, if tank is full when trip lever breaks, it appears to not be filling.

**Fix:**
- Replace flush handle ($10-20)
- Reconnect chain to flapper
- Adjust chain length (slight slack when handle is not pressed)

## Preventive Maintenance

**Keep toilet filling properly:**

**Every 6 months:**
- Flush out sediment from tank
- Clean fill valve screen
- Check all connections for leaks
- Inspect flapper for wear

**Annually:**
- Replace flapper (cheap insurance against leaks)
- Clean mineral deposits from tank components
- Check water supply line for cracks or wear

**Every 5 years:**
- Consider replacing fill valve proactively
- Replace water supply line

**Water quality matters:**
- Hard water accelerates mineral buildup
- Consider water softener if severe hard water
- Flush fill valve more frequently in hard water areas

## Troubleshooting Persistent Problems

### Tank Fills But Water Level Too Low

**Causes:**
- Float set too low
- Fill valve adjustment wrong

**Fix:**
Adjust float higher to allow more water in tank

### Tank Fills But Immediately Drains

**Cause:**
Flapper not sealing

**Fix:**
Replace flapper and clean seal surface

### Water Won't Stop Running

**Causes:**
- Float stuck or set too high
- Fill valve failed to shut off
- Water flowing over overflow tube constantly

**Fix:**
- Adjust float lower
- Replace fill valve
- Ensure overflow tube isn't damaged

### Noisy Filling

**Causes:**
- Water pressure too high
- Fill valve type creates noise
- Loose pipes vibrating

**Fix:**
- Install pressure regulator if pressure over 80 PSI
- Replace with quiet-fill valve
- Secure water supply pipes

## When to Call a Plumber

**DIY appropriate for:**
- Fill valve replacement
- Float adjustment
- Flapper replacement
- Water supply line replacement
- Cleaning mineral deposits

**Call plumber for:**
- No water anywhere in house (unless confirmed outage)
- Tank leaks from cracks (may need tank or toilet replacement)
- Shut-off valve won't close or is damaged
- Supply line leaks from wall
- You're uncomfortable with any repair
- Repeated failures after DIY attempts

**Plumber costs:**
- Service call: $100-150
- Fill valve replacement: $150-250 installed
- Full toilet replacement: $250-500 installed

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How long should toilet tank take to fill?**

1-2 minutes typically. Over 5 minutes indicates problem (clogged fill valve or low water pressure).

**Q: Why does my toilet tank fill slowly in winter?**

Cold water is denser and flows slightly slower. May also indicate mineral buildup that's more apparent with cold water.

**Q: Can I use universal fill valve for any toilet?**

Yes, most modern fill valves (like Fluidmaster 400A) are universal and adjustable for height and water level.

**Q: Should I replace both fill valve and flapper at same time?**

Good practice when replacing either. Both are inexpensive and doing them together saves labor later.

**Q: My toilet worked fine for years and suddenly won't fill. What happened?**

Most likely water supply valve partially closed (bumped) or fill valve suddenly clogged. Check valve position first.

**Q: Is it normal for fill valve to make noise?**

Slight sound is normal. Loud hissing, hammering, or vibrating indicates problem or high water pressure.

## Conclusion

Toilet tanks that won't fill usually have simple causes: partially closed water supply valve, clogged fill valve, or stuck float mechanism. Start troubleshooting by confirming the water supply valve is fully open—this surprisingly common issue resolves many "broken" toilets instantly.

For slow-filling toilets, cleaning the fill valve screen often restores proper flow. When cleaning doesn't help or the valve is over 5 years old, replacement is straightforward and inexpensive. Universal fill valves cost $10-30 and install in 30-45 minutes with basic tools.

Regular maintenance—cleaning mineral deposits, replacing flappers every 1-2 years, and flushing out sediment—prevents most toilet tank filling problems. These simple tasks take minutes but avoid the inconvenience and potential water waste of a malfunctioning toilet.

When basic troubleshooting doesn't identify the problem, professional plumbers can diagnose issues with supply lines, shut-off valves, or water pressure that may not be apparent to homeowners. However, the vast majority of toilet tank filling problems are easily resolved with DIY repairs.
