# Door Won't Latch Properly: How to Fix Doors That Won't Stay Closed

A door that won't latch is annoying and potentially compromises security and privacy. Most latching failures result from door misalignment caused by settling foundations, loose hinges, temperature/humidity changes, or strike plate issues rather than actual latch mechanism failure. The door frame hasn't moved—the door has shifted position so the latch bolt no longer lines up with the strike plate hole. This guide covers all common causes and provides simple adjustments to restore proper latching.

## Understanding Door Latch Components

**Parts involved in latching:**

**Latch bolt:** The spring-loaded piece that extends from door edge into strike plate hole

**Strike plate:** Metal plate on door frame with hole for latch bolt

**Hinges:** Connect door to frame; control door positioning

**Door frame:** The stationary part attached to wall

**Latch problems occur when** latch bolt and strike plate hole don't align properly.

## Diagnosing the Problem

Before fixing, determine what's misaligned:

### Visual Inspection

1. Close door slowly and watch latch bolt
2. Does it hit above, below, or to side of strike plate hole?
3. Does it hit strike plate face without entering hole?
4. Does it not reach strike plate at all?

### The Crayon Test

**For subtle misalignment:**

1. Color end of latch bolt with crayon or lipstick
2. Close door (it won't latch)
3. Open door
4. Look at strike plate—colored mark shows where bolt is hitting
5. Measure how far off from hole

This shows exactly how much adjustment is needed.

### Gap Check

**Look at gap around door when closed:**

1. Should be even (about 1/8 inch all around)
2. If gap is uneven, door has shifted
3. Wider gap at top: Hinges pulled away or settled
4. Wider gap at bottom: Door sagging on hinges
5. Wider gap on latch side: Door pulled away from frame

## Quick Fix: Strike Plate Adjustment

**For minor misalignment** (1/8 inch or less):

### Option 1: File the Strike Plate

1. Remove strike plate screws
2. Remove strike plate
3. Use metal file to enlarge hole in direction needed
4. File opening (not just hole depth)
5. Test fit
6. Continue filing until latch enters smoothly
7. Reinstall strike plate
8. Test door

**Works when:** Latch is very close but just misses hole

### Option 2: Reposition Strike Plate

**For larger misalignment** (up to 1/4 inch):

1. Close door and mark where latch actually hits
2. Remove strike plate
3. Fill old screw holes with wood putty or toothpicks + glue
4. Let dry
5. Position strike plate in new location
6. Mark new screw holes
7. Drill pilot holes
8. Install strike plate in new position
9. May need to chisel deeper mortise if moving significantly
10. Test door

## Fix Sagging Hinges

**Most common cause of latch problems** especially on older or heavy doors.

### Symptoms:
- Door appears to sag
- Gap wider at top of door or latch side
- Latch bolt hits below strike plate hole
- Door feels heavy or drags on floor

### Solution 1: Tighten Hinge Screws

**Simplest fix—try this first:**

1. Open door fully
2. Tighten all screws on all hinges (both door side and frame side)
3. If screws spin without tightening, holes are stripped (see Solution 2)
4. Close door and test latch

**Use longer screws:**

Many hinge screws are short (3/4 inch). Replacing with longer screws (2-1/2 to 3 inches) on frame side:

1. Reaches into wall framing beyond door jamb
2. Pulls door tighter to frame
3. Prevents future sagging

**Do this on one screw per hinge** (top and middle hinges). Long screws in all holes can split wood.

### Solution 2: Fix Stripped Screw Holes

If screws won't tighten because holes are stripped:

**Method 1: Toothpick/Matchstick Fix**

1. Remove hinge
2. Fill stripped holes with wooden toothpicks or matchsticks
3. Add wood glue
4. Break off flush with surface
5. Let dry
6. Reinstall hinge—screws will bite into new wood

**Method 2: Larger Screws**

1. Use next larger diameter screw
2. May need to drill pilot hole to prevent splitting
3. Don't go too large—can split jamb

**Method 3: Wall Anchors**

1. Insert wall anchors into stripped holes
2. Reinstall screws into anchors
3. Provides new grip in soft wood

### Solution 3: Shim the Hinges

If door is aligned but latch still misses:

**Add cardboard shims behind hinges:**

**If latch hits high** (above strike plate hole):
- Shim bottom hinge
- This raises door slightly

**If latch hits low:**
- Shim top hinge
- This lowers door slightly

**Process:**

1. Remove hinge
2. Cut thin cardboard (cereal box works) to match hinge mortise shape
3. Place in mortise
4. Reinstall hinge on top of shim
5. May need multiple layers for sufficient adjustment
6. Test and adjust

**Proper shimming** fixes alignment without visible changes to door position.

## Fix Warped Doors

Wood doors can warp from humidity changes.

**Testing for warp:**

1. Close door
2. Look along edge—should be straight
3. Bow or twist indicates warp

**Minor warp fixes:**

**For doors that bow:**

1. Adjust hinges as described above
2. May need to enlarge strike plate hole to accommodate
3. Ensure door is sealed/painted on all edges to prevent moisture absorption

**For severe warp:**

**Planing the edge:**

1. Mark where latch hits
2. Remove door
3. Plane edge to remove high spot
4. Sand smooth
5. Seal/paint edge
6. Reinstall

**Note:** Severe warps often indicate moisture problems. Address humidity control.

## Fix Latch Mechanism Itself

Less common, but latches can fail.

**Symptoms:**
- Latch bolt doesn't extend fully
- Latch bolt sticks
- Latch bolt wobbles loosely

**Solutions:**

### Clean and Lubricate

1. Spray WD-40 or silicone spray into latch mechanism
2. Work latch in and out repeatedly
3. Wipe excess
4. Test operation

### Tighten Latch Assembly

1. Remove door knob/handle
2. Tighten screws holding latch in door
3. Reassemble
4. Test

### Replace Latch

If broken:

1. Remove door knob/handle
2. Remove screws holding latch in door edge
3. Pull out old latch
4. Measure and purchase matching replacement
5. Install new latch
6. Reinstall knob/handle

**Cost:** Replacement latches $5-20

## Seasonal Adjustment

Wood doors expand and contract with humidity.

**Summer:** Doors swell, may stick or not close easily

**Winter:** Doors shrink, gaps appear, may not latch well

**Solutions:**

**For seasonal issues:**
- Adjust strike plate position seasonally (mark positions for easy adjustment)
- Consider installing spring-loaded strike plates that adjust automatically
- Seal doors properly to minimize expansion/contraction

**Prevention:**
- Paint/seal all six sides of door (including top and bottom edges)
- Maintain consistent indoor humidity (40-50%)
- Use storm doors for exterior doors

## Special Cases

### Doors That Won't Stay Open

Different problem but related:

**Cause:** Door not hanging level (floor not level or hinge problems)

**Test:** Door swings closed or open on its own when released halfway

**Fix:**

1. Check hinge screws (tighten)
2. Shim hinges to level door
3. Adjust hinges so door hangs perfectly vertical
4. May need to plane bottom of door if floor is sloped

### Bifold Doors Won't Latch

**Common issues:**
- Pivot pins misaligned
- Track bent or misaligned
- Latch magnet weak or misaligned

**Fix:**
- Adjust pivot pins
- Realign track
- Reposition magnetic catch
- Replace weak magnets

### Sliding Doors Won't Latch

**Issues:**
- Door off track
- Rollers worn
- Frame misaligned

**Fix:**
- Clean track
- Adjust roller height
- Ensure door properly seated on track
- May need roller replacement

## Preventive Maintenance

**Prevent latch problems:**

**Annually:**
- Tighten all hinge screws
- Check door alignment
- Lubricate hinges with 3-in-1 oil or similar
- Check strike plate alignment

**Seasonally:**
- Check for new gaps or binding
- Make minor adjustments before problems worsen

**As needed:**
- Paint/seal all door edges
- Fix minor issues immediately before they become major

## When to Call a Professional

**DIY appropriate for:**
- Tightening hinges
- Adjusting strike plates
- Minor shimming
- Latch lubrication

**Call professional for:**
- Door replacement needed
- Frame damage requiring repair
- Complex mortise lock adjustments
- You're uncomfortable with tools
- Repeated failures after DIY attempts

**Carpenter costs:** Typically $100-200 for door adjustment

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Why does my door latch sometimes but not other times?**

Temperature and humidity changes cause slight expansion/contraction. Adjust strike plate to accommodate, or seal door better to minimize expansion.

**Q: I fixed the latch but now the door sticks. What happened?**

Over-adjusted. Door slightly too far into frame. Back off adjustments slightly or plane door edge minimally.

**Q: Can I just remove the strike plate?**

Not recommended. Door won't stay closed without strike plate. Also reduces security significantly.

**Q: My exterior door won't latch in winter. Why?**

Door contracted in cold weather. Adjust strike plate inward or add shims behind hinges to move door slightly.

**Q: Should I replace the whole lockset if latch doesn't work?**

Not usually necessary. Latch mechanism alone usually replaceable ($5-20) versus full lockset ($30-100+).

**Q: How do I know if my door is warped?**

Close door and look along edge from hinge side. Should be straight. Visible bow indicates warp.

## Conclusion

Doors that won't latch properly usually require simple hinge or strike plate adjustments rather than hardware replacement. Start with the easiest fixes: tighten all hinge screws (especially using longer screws into wall framing) and test whether this resolves the issue. If not, the crayon test shows exactly where the latch is hitting, guiding strike plate adjustment.

For most homeowners, repositioning the strike plate or shimming hinges resolves latching problems in under 30 minutes with basic tools. These adjustments compensate for natural house settling, seasonal door expansion/contraction, and normal wear on hinges over time.

Maintain doors proactively by tightening hinges annually, sealing all edges against moisture, and making small adjustments when problems first appear. This prevents minor alignment issues from becoming frustrating daily problems requiring more extensive repair.
