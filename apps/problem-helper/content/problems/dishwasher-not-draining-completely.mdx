# Dishwasher Not Draining Completely: 8 Common Causes & Fixes

Opening your dishwasher to find standing water pooled at the bottom is unpleasant and indicates a drainage problem that needs immediate attention. A dishwasher that won't drain properly leaves dishes dirty, creates odors, and can lead to more serious appliance damage if ignored. Fortunately, most drainage issues have simple causes you can fix yourself without calling an expensive repairperson.

## Why Is There Water in the Bottom of My Dishwasher?

A small amount of water (about a cup) at the very bottom near the drain is normal—this water seals the pump and prevents odors. However, if you can see water covering the bottom or pooled around the filter, your dishwasher isn't draining correctly.

Dishwashers drain through a series of components: the filter catches food particles, water flows into the sump, the drain pump pushes water through the drain hose, and finally the water exits either directly into your sink drain or through your garbage disposal. When any component in this chain fails or clogs, drainage stops.

Common causes of dishwasher drainage failure include clogged filters, obstructed drain hoses, faulty drain pumps, blocked garbage disposals, and installation errors. Each cause has specific symptoms and solutions detailed below.

## 8 Common Causes and How to Fix Them

### Cause 1: Clogged or Dirty Filter

The most frequent cause of poor drainage is a clogged filter. The filter traps food debris to prevent pump damage, but when it becomes clogged, water can't flow through to drain.

**Symptoms:** Standing water, poor cleaning performance, food particles on dishes, unpleasant odors.

**Solution:**
1. Remove the bottom dish rack to access the filter assembly
2. Twist and lift the cylindrical filter (varies by model—check your manual)
3. Inspect the filter screen and filter cup for food buildup
4. Rinse under hot running water, using a soft brush to remove stuck debris
5. Check the filter housing in the dishwasher for trapped items
6. Reassemble and test

Clean your filter monthly or bi-weekly if you rinse dishes minimally before loading. This simple maintenance prevents most drainage problems.

### Cause 2: Blocked Drain Hose

The drain hose carries water from the dishwasher to your sink drain or garbage disposal. Kinks, clogs, or improper installation can prevent water from exiting.

**Symptoms:** Water drains slowly or not at all, gurgling sounds during drain cycle, water backs up into sink when dishwasher drains.

**Solution:**
1. Disconnect power to the dishwasher at the breaker
2. Pull out the dishwasher carefully to access the drain hose (usually at the back)
3. Inspect the hose for kinks—straighten any bent sections
4. Feel along the hose for hard spots indicating clogs
5. Disconnect the hose (have towels ready for water)
6. Flush the hose with hot water or use a plumber's snake for stubborn clogs
7. Ensure the hose creates a "high loop" or has an air gap—it should rise higher than the connection point before dropping down to prevent backflow
8. Reconnect everything and test

### Cause 3: Garbage Disposal Knockout Plug

If your dishwasher connects to a new garbage disposal, the knockout plug must be removed. This plastic plug blocks the dishwasher drain port inside the disposal and is a frequent DIY installation oversight.

**Symptoms:** New installation or recent disposal replacement, dishwasher fills but won't drain, disposal works fine.

**Solution:**
1. Turn off power to the disposal and dishwasher
2. Disconnect the drain hose from the disposal inlet
3. Using a screwdriver and hammer, knock out the plastic plug from inside the disposal inlet tube
4. Retrieve the plug from inside the disposal—don't leave it to damage the disposal
5. Reconnect the drain hose
6. Test the system

This is a one-time fix but affects many new disposal installations where installers forget this critical step.

### Cause 4: Clogged Garbage Disposal

Even if the knockout plug is removed, a clogged garbage disposal can prevent dishwasher drainage since the dishwasher drains through it.

**Symptoms:** Disposal drains slowly, standing water in disposal, food backup, dishwasher won't drain when disposal is full.

**Solution:**
1. Turn off power to disposal
2. Check the disposal for visible clogs at the drain opening
3. Clear any food debris
4. Pour baking soda and vinegar into disposal, let sit 10 minutes, flush with hot water
5. Run disposal with water to ensure it drains freely
6. Test dishwasher drainage

If the disposal still doesn't drain, you may need to snake the drain pipe or address a sink plumbing clog unrelated to the dishwasher.

### Cause 5: Faulty Drain Pump

The drain pump actively pushes water out of the dishwasher. If it fails, water simply sits in the tub. Drain pumps can fail from age, debris damage, or motor burnout.

**Symptoms:** No humming or grinding during drain cycle, water doesn't drain at all, error codes related to drainage, burning smell during drain attempts.

**Solution (diagnosis):**
1. Remove the filter to access the pump area
2. Check for objects jamming the pump impeller (glass shards, bones, twist ties)
3. Manually rotate the impeller—it should spin freely
4. Run a drain cycle and listen—a working pump makes a distinct humming sound

**Solution (repair):**
If the pump is jammed, remove the obstruction. If the pump motor is burned out or damaged, replacement is necessary. Dishwasher drain pumps cost $50-100 and require some disassembly to replace. Many homeowners can handle this repair with a video tutorial specific to their dishwasher model, or you can hire a professional.

### Cause 6: Clogged Air Gap

Some installations include an air gap—a small cylindrical fixture on the sink or counter-top that prevents backflow contamination. These can clog with debris and prevent drainage.

**Symptoms:** Water overflows from the air gap, dishwasher drains slowly, gurgling from air gap during drain cycle.

**Solution:**
1. Remove the air gap cover (usually twists off)
2. Check for food debris or buildup inside
3. Clean thoroughly with hot water and a small brush
4. Check both hoses connected to the air gap for clogs
5. Reassemble and test

Not all dishwashers have air gaps—check your sink area for this small chrome or plastic device.

### Cause 7: Check Valve Stuck Closed

The check valve (also called a drain valve or flapper valve) prevents drained water from flowing back into the dishwasher. If it sticks in the closed position, water can't drain.

**Symptoms:** Water drains partially but stops, intermittent drainage issues, standing water after some cycles but not others.

**Solution:**
The check valve location varies by model. It's typically near the drain pump and may be part of the drain hose assembly or pump itself. Access it by removing the bottom panel and inspecting the drain path. If the valve is stuck, broken, or coated with grime, clean or replace it. This repair is model-specific—consult your dishwasher manual or online videos for your exact model.

### Cause 8: Improper Installation

New dishwasher installations sometimes have drainage problems due to incorrect hose routing, missing high loops, or drain hose positioned too high above the floor.

**Symptoms:** New installation, water drains partially, slow drainage from day one.

**Solution:**
1. Verify the drain hose rises in a high loop (highest point under the counter) before dropping to the drain
2. Confirm the drain connection point is no more than 8-10 inches above the floor
3. Check that the hose isn't excessively long (more than 12 feet) which strains the pump
4. Ensure proper hose attachment at both ends with secure clamps

Installation problems require disassembly and potential replumbing, which may warrant professional help if you're uncomfortable with the modifications.

## Quick Diagnostic Checklist

Before diving into repairs, run through this checklist to narrow down the cause:

1. **Is there a cycle interrupted error?** Check the control panel for error codes and consult your manual.

2. **Does water drain at all?** If none drains, suspect pump failure or complete blockage. Partial drainage suggests filter or hose issues.

3. **Is the disposal clear?** Run the disposal and confirm it drains freely.

4. **When did the problem start?** Recent installations or repairs often indicate related issues.

5. **Do you hear the drain pump running?** A silent drain cycle suggests electrical or pump problems.

6. **How much water remains?** A couple inches suggests partial clogs; a full tub suggests complete drainage failure.

## Prevention Tips

Prevent future drainage problems with these maintenance practices:

**Scrape plates thoroughly** before loading. While modern dishwashers handle food residue, large particles still clog filters and pumps.

**Run hot water** at the sink before starting the dishwasher to ensure hot water enters immediately, improving detergent performance and debris dissolution.

**Clean the filter** monthly. This single maintenance task prevents most dishwasher problems.

**Run a cleaning cycle** monthly using dishwasher cleaner or a vinegar rinse (run empty cycle with a cup of white vinegar in the bottom) to remove buildup.

**Check spray arms** for clogs. Remove and rinse spray arms to ensure water pressure remains strong.

**Avoid overloading** the dishwasher. Overcrowding prevents proper water circulation and can trap debris in the filter.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is it normal to have a little water in the bottom of my dishwasher?**

Yes. A small amount of water (about 1 cup) is normal and necessary. It seals the pump and prevents odors. If water levels exceed this or you can see water sitting around the filter, drainage is incomplete.

**Q: Can I run my dishwasher if it's not draining?**

No. Running a dishwasher with standing water can overflow during the next fill cycle, damage the pump by recirculating dirty water, and potentially cause water damage to flooring. Fix the drainage issue before using it again.

**Q: Why does my dishwasher drain into the sink?**

This indicates a clog in the shared drain pipe or a failure of the air gap/high loop system. The dishwasher and sink share drainage, so blockages cause backups. Check the disposal, air gap, and drain hose routing.

**Q: How do I force my dishwasher to drain?**

If your dishwasher won't initiate a drain cycle, try: (1) Run a "Cancel/Drain" cycle from the control panel; (2) Manually advance through cycles until it reaches drain; (3) As a last resort, use a cup or wet-dry vacuum to manually remove standing water before servicing.

**Q: Can I use Drano in my dishwasher?**

Never use Drano or chemical drain cleaners in a dishwasher. These harsh chemicals can damage pumps, seals, and hoses, potentially causing leaks or expensive component failure. Use manual cleaning, vinegar, or baking soda instead.

**Q: How much does it cost to fix a dishwasher that won't drain?**

DIY fixes cost $0-$20 for cleaning supplies. Drain pump replacement (DIY) costs $50-100. Professional service calls run $100-250 for diagnosis and minor repairs, or $200-400 for pump replacement including labor.

## When to Call a Professional

Most dishwasher drainage issues are DIY-friendly, but call a professional if:

- You've tried all troubleshooting steps without success
- The drain pump needs replacement and you're uncomfortable with appliance repair
- There are electrical issues or error codes you can't resolve
- Water is leaking onto floors, indicating a more serious problem
- The dishwasher is under warranty (DIY repairs may void coverage)

Professional appliance repair typically costs $100-300 for drainage issues, which may be worthwhile for complex problems or expensive dishwashers you want properly serviced.

## Conclusion

A dishwasher that won't drain completely usually has a simple, fixable cause. Start with the easiest solutions—cleaning the filter and checking the disposal—before moving to more complex diagnostics like checking the drain pump or hose routing.

Regular filter cleaning and proper loading habits prevent most drainage problems. Taking ten minutes monthly for basic maintenance keeps your dishwasher draining properly for years and saves hundreds in potential repair costs.

If DIY troubleshooting doesn't resolve the issue, don't hesitate to call a professional. Water damage from continued use of a malfunctioning dishwasher can cost far more than a simple service call.
