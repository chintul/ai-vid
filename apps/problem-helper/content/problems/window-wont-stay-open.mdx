# Window Won't Stay Open: Fix Broken Window Balance Springs

A window that won't stay open is frustrating and dangerous—falling windows can cause injury or damage. Most cases involve broken or worn balance mechanisms that counterweight the sash. Understanding your window type and balance system helps determine the right fix, from simple spring replacement to professional service.

## Types of Window Balance Systems

**Spiral balances:** Common in newer double-hung windows. Metal spiral rod inside tube provides tension. When broken, window falls closed immediately.

**Block and tackle:** Pulleys, cords, and concealed weights. Often in older double-hung windows. Broken cords cause window to drop or not stay in position.

**Spring balances:** Coiled springs inside side channels. Wear out over time, gradually losing tension until window won't hold position.

**Cam pivot:** Friction-based system in some modern windows. When worn, window slides down slowly rather than staying put.

Identifying your system determines repair approach.

## Quick Diagnosis

**Test the problem:**
1. Open window halfway
2. Release slowly—does it fall immediately or drift down?
3. Does one side seem heavier than other?
4. Can you see broken cords or springs?

Immediate falling indicates complete balance failure. Slow drifting suggests worn but not broken balances.

## Fixing Spiral Balances

Most common in modern windows, relatively easy to replace.

**Steps:**
1. Remove window sash (usually tilts in for removal)
2. Locate spiral balance tubes on each side
3. Unhook damaged balance from sash
4. Note balance specifications (tube length, color code for strength)
5. Purchase replacement balances (hardware stores or online)
6. Install new balances following manufacturer directions
7. Reinstall sash and test

**Cost:** $15-40 per balance. Most windows need two balances replaced as pairs.

## Fixing Block and Tackle Systems

Traditional system requiring more involved repair.

**Diagnosis:** Open side jamb covers to access pulleys and cords.

**Repair:**
1. Remove interior stops to free sash
2. Lift out sash carefully
3. Access weight pocket (remove panel or cut opening)
4. Retrieve broken cord and weight
5. Thread new sash cord over pulley
6. Attach one end to weight, other to sash
7. Adjust tension for proper counterbalance
8. Reinstall sash and stops

**Materials:** Sash cord ($5-10), weights if missing ($10-20).

This repair requires patience and moderate DIY skills. Many homeowners hire professionals for block and tackle repairs.

## Temporary Fixes

When immediate permanent repair isn't possible:

**Window props:** Cut wooden dowel or stick to length. Prop window open securely. Not elegant but prevents falls and provides ventilation.

**Locking positions:** Some windows have friction locks at intervals. Use these to hold window at specific heights.

**Velcro strips:** Attach industrial-strength Velcro to sash and frame. Provides temporary holding force for light windows.

These are stopgaps only—broken balances will continue degrading.

## When to Replace vs. Repair

**Repair if:**
- Window under 15 years old
- Only balances need replacement
- Frame and sash in good condition
- Matching replacement parts available

**Replace if:**
- Window over 25 years old with multiple problems
- Frame rotted or damaged
- Single-pane windows (energy inefficient)
- Repeated balance failures
- Replacement cost approaches window replacement cost

Modern replacement windows cost $300-700 installed and include new balances, better energy efficiency, and warranties.

## DIY vs. Professional Service

**DIY appropriate for:**
- Spiral balance replacement (straightforward)
- Younger windows with accessible mechanisms
- If you're comfortable with moderate home repairs

**Hire professional for:**
- Block and tackle systems (complex)
- Older windows requiring custom parts
- Multiple windows needing service
- Frame or structural issues

Window repair services typically charge $100-200 per window for balance replacement including labor.

## Prevention Tips

Extend balance life with proper care:

- Operate windows smoothly (don't force or slam)
- Keep tracks and channels clean
- Lubricate moving parts annually
- Address problems early before complete failure
- Replace balances in pairs (if one fails, the other is close behind)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I use WD-40 on window balances?**

For metal parts, yes, but use silicone spray for better results on springs and plastic components. Clean first, then lubricate lightly.

**Q: Why does only one side of my window fall?**

One balance failed while the other still works. Replace both—when one fails, the other is worn and will fail soon.

**Q: My window falls very slowly. Is that bad?**

Yes—balances are worn and will eventually fail completely. Replace now while window is still functional rather than waiting for emergency situation.

**Q: How long do window balances last?**

Spiral balances: 10-15 years. Block and tackle: 20-30 years if cords maintained. Spring balances: 15-20 years. Lifespan varies with use frequency and quality.

**Q: Can I replace just one balance?**

Technically yes, but not recommended. Windows need balanced tension on both sides. Always replace balances in pairs.

**Q: Are replacement balances universal?**

No—balances are specific to window size, weight, and brand. Measure carefully and ideally bring old balance to store for matching.

## Safety Considerations

Falling windows are hazardous:

- Never position yourself under an unsupported window
- Don't let children operate windows with balance problems
- Use props immediately when problem discovered
- Repair promptly—don't procrastinate on this safety issue

## Conclusion

Windows that won't stay open need balance mechanism attention. Most modern spiral balance replacements are straightforward DIY projects taking 1-2 hours per window. Older block and tackle systems require more skill but remain repairable.

Don't ignore falling windows—they're safety hazards and prevent proper ventilation. Addressing balance problems promptly prevents injuries and maintains home comfort and air circulation.

For windows over 20 years old with multiple issues, consider full replacement for better energy efficiency and modern convenience features alongside solving the balance problem permanently.
